Dev Log #0004 — Uncharted Rally… 2?

11 February 2026 / 2 min read / Oliver Creese

I have completely rewritten my chunk generation in C++ and made some optimisation gains, as well as opening pathways for a better regeneration. I will continue to refine it as I go. I have made big progress in making autonomous drivers and have created an AI driver that can throttle and steer along a spline.

C++ Rewrite

My main reason for doing this was to dive into some C++ and to apply its powerful capabilities to a task that would actually be able to utilise it. Since all my logic was already in Blueprint, most of it was just translation — however, I made optimisation gains since now I am able to manage memory. I have replaced my original "generate all chunks right at this very moment on the game thread" with a slower but more economical approach. Essentially I am storing all the chunk positions in a queue and then gradually generating them through a timer. This way I don't hog the game thread and other processes can still execute. I have also stored all chunks inside an array of pointers, so that when I need to regenerate I can do a similar process.

AI Drivers

I have created a simple AI driver that maintains throttle and steers its way onto a spline path. It is steering by using traditional calculation of finding the angle between the closest spline point and the car's forward vector, then mapping that to the steering input. Through some helpful Blueprint nodes which return the closest position on a spline at a given distance, I then just need to find the delta angle between the car's forward vector and the vector from the front of the car to the point on the spline.

What's Next?

My next task is to bake the chunks into a single static mesh. Since I have switched to C++ this will be much easier.
