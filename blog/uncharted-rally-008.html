Dev Log · Uncharted Rally

Uncharted Rally #008

22 July 2026 / 2 min read / Oliver Creese

Dynamic per-wheel traction, six new track surfaces, and the kick-up particle and sound effects that bring them to life.

Dynamic Traction

I've implemented a system that controls the friction multiplier per wheel depending on whether that wheel is actually on the track. If a wheel drifts off the track its friction multiplier is reduced, which forces players to stay on the racing line for optimal grip. While on the track, friction is instead dictated by the current weather condition and the tyre compound in use. This feeds directly into the new surface kick-up effect system, which uses the same per-wheel surface data to control when particles should be emitted.

Different Track Surfaces

There are now six different track surfaces that dictate how the car interacts with the ground. Each surface has its own physical material applied to the terrain on generation, and the surface type is randomised per stage and shown in the pre-stage car setup screen so players can customise their car for each unique stage. This also drives the particle effects, since each surface type has its own dedicated effect.

Kick-Up Effects

The kick-up effect is emitted from each car's tyres to give a sense of power and mass as it moves across a surface. Depending on the car's drivetrain, the effect is more potent at the front or the rear. Using a fixed update cycle, I check per wheel what the Chaos Wheeled Vehicle component has collected that frame — whether the car is skidding, whether the wheel is slipping, and its RPM — and feed that into the particle system to control the velocity and quantity of particles emitted.

Sounds

Sound is driven by a pre-made FMOD sound bank, integrated using the FMOD for Unreal plugin, which lets me feed runtime data into the bank for accurate audio. The bank allows custom tuning of turbo and supercharger sounds per car, giving each vehicle a unique voice. There are still a few hitches with the engine sound caused by the engine simulation itself that I need to figure out.

Current Issues

The engine sound work exposed one of the more glaring issues with the Chaos Wheeled Vehicle component: the engine RPM cut-off/soft limiter it applies to prevent oversteer under acceleration. It really detracts from the experience, because without control over that RPM cut-off the sound always cuts off abruptly, which sounds jarring. I'm looking into ways to override this without breaking the car's handling, as well as looking at custom or third-party vehicle components.
